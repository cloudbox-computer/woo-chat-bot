Yes — and I would make ticket creation a controlled tool, not something the AI can freely decide to execute.

For your multi-tenant chatbot, the flow should be:

Customer
   │
   ▼
Chatbot
   │
   │ "I'm having a problem with my order"
   ▼
AI determines a support ticket is appropriate
   │
   ▼
create_ticket()
   │
   ├── Validate tenant
   ├── Create ticket in Supabase
   ├── Generate ticket reference
   └── Trigger email
          │
          ▼
      Tenant's email
Example

Customer says:

My necklace arrived damaged.

The AI can respond:

I'm sorry about that. I can create a support ticket for you so the Ivy & Pearls team can look into this. Would you like me to create one?

Customer:

Yes.

Then the AI calls:

create_ticket({
  category: "damaged_item",
  subject: "Damaged necklace received",
  description: "...",
  customer_name: "...",
  customer_email: "..."
})

Your Edge Function, not the AI, does the actual work.

It generates something like:

IP-2026-004821

Then:

Supabase
   │
   ├── INSERT ticket
   │
   └── trigger email
            │
            ▼
       tenant email

And the customer gets:

Your support ticket has been created.
Reference: IP-2026-004821

The Ivy & Pearls team has been notified and will review your request.

🔐 Make the ticket tool very restricted

I'd expose only:

create_ticket()

initially.

The AI should not be able to:

delete_ticket()
change_tenant()
send_arbitrary_email()
change_ticket_owner()

The Edge Function controls those things.

For example:

create_ticket()
        │
        ▼
Edge Function
        │
        ├── tenant = authenticated chatbot tenant
        ├── validate customer details
        ├── validate category
        ├── generate reference
        ├── insert ticket
        └── queue notification

The AI doesn't get to specify:

tenant_id
email recipient
ticket reference

Those are generated by your backend.

📧 Email the tenant automatically

I'd actually separate email sending from ticket creation.

Rather than:

create_ticket()
   ↓
send email

I'd do:

create_ticket()
   ↓
database transaction
   ↓
ticket created
   ↓
event/queue
   ↓
send_ticket_email()

That way if the email provider temporarily fails, the ticket still exists.

You can retry the email without creating a duplicate ticket.

Since you've already been working with Resend, it's a natural fit here.

The tenant email could look something like:

New support ticket — IP-2026-004821

A new customer support ticket has been created.

Customer: Jane Smith
Email: jane@example.com
Category: Damaged item
Priority: Normal

Issue:
Customer reports that their necklace arrived damaged.

Ticket: IP-2026-004821

[View ticket]

🧠 The chatbot should know when to create one

I'd define explicit rules rather than simply telling the AI:

"Create tickets when appropriate."

For example, ticket-worthy issues:

✓ Damaged product
✓ Missing order
✓ Wrong product received
✓ Product defect
✓ Delivery problem
✓ Refund problem
✓ Payment problem
✓ Order problem
✓ Customer complaint
✓ Customer explicitly requests support
✓ Issue requires human intervention

But:

❌ "What necklaces do you sell?"
❌ "How much is delivery?"
❌ "What's your return policy?"
❌ "Do you have gold earrings?"

should be answered normally.

⚠️ Don't let it create tickets too easily

I'd add a confirmation step.

For example:

Customer:

My order arrived damaged.

AI:

I'm sorry to hear that. I can create a support ticket for the Ivy & Pearls team. Would you like me to do that?

Customer:

Yes.

AI → create_ticket()

This prevents accidental tickets.

However, if the customer explicitly says:

"Please open a ticket for this."

you can skip the extra confirmation.

🛡️ This also fits our strict tenant architecture

The ticket is permanently associated with the tenant:

chatbot
   ↓
tenant_id
   ↓
ticket
   ↓
tenant email

So if the chatbot is embedded on Ivy & Pearls:

IP-2026-004821

goes only to the Ivy & Pearls support address.

Another tenant:

NTM-2026-000143

goes only to the NTM Associates support address.

The customer should never be able to manipulate the destination email through the chat.
